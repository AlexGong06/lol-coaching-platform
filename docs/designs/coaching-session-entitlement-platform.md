## Problem Statement

Alex coaches League of Legends players. Today students reach out over Discord DMs,
agree on a time by hand, and pay through an informal channel. There is no booking
surface, no payment gate, and no automatic control over who can enter the Discord
coaching space or when.

A platform is being built to replace that manual flow. It has a second purpose: it
is a project Alex is submitting to **Corgi** (corgi.insure) around a software
engineer screening on **2026-11-16**. As of 2026-11-13 Alex has decided it will
**not** be the primary thing discussed in that screening, which removes the hard
deadline from the design without removing the audience. Corgi is a full-stack
insurance carrier; its renters-insurance platform Beagle automates enrollment,
billing, compliance, claims, and physical fulfillment. Corgi's job description asks
for state machines with real financial consequences, messy third-party data
ingestion, real money flows, integration-heavy systems with monitoring, and
end-to-end ownership of production systems.

So the deliverable has to satisfy two readers at once: a student who wants to book
and attend a session, and an engineering team deciding whether to hire. The second
reader is no longer on a clock, which means the project can be finished properly
rather than to a date.

## Demand Evidence

- Alex has coached students before this platform existed. Verbatim: *"I've coached
  people before without this platform so the product should absolutely be usable by
  a potential student."* This is behavioral, not hypothetical — the coaching already
  happens and already gets paid for.
- The current workflow is Discord DMs plus manual payment, which means the pain
  points are observed rather than imagined: no-shows, "did my payment go through,"
  timezone confusion, and manually letting people into the server.
- The evaluating audience is named and specific: Corgi's CTO and engineering team,
  with a known job description and a known date.

What is *not* established: volume. Nothing in this session established how many
students per week, so the design assumes low concurrency and does not invest in
scale. Correctness under concurrency is still enforced at the database level
because it is cheap there, not because volume demands it.

## Status Quo

Students message Alex on Discord, the two of them settle on a time by hand, and
money moves informally. Discord server access is granted manually and never
revoked. Nothing records what was agreed, nothing confirms payment against a
session, and nothing ends access when a session ends.

Every feature in the recommended approach maps to a specific failure of this
workflow. That mapping is the README's opening paragraph, and it is the most
credible framing the project has: this replaces something real, and the thing it
replaces can be described precisely.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Two users, one system.**

1. **The student** — a League player who wants a one-hour session, picks a slot,
   pays, and gets into the coaching voice channel at the right time without
   messaging anyone.
2. **The evaluator** — a Corgi engineer who reads the repository, may click a live
   link, and is deciding whether Alex can own a production system where mistakes
   cost money.

**Narrowest wedge:** a student can go from landing page to *inside the voice
channel at the right time* without Alex touching anything. That single path — book,
sign in with Discord, pay, receive a working join link at window open and nothing
before it — is the whole product. Everything else is optional.

**What the wedge no longer claims.** Access ending is not automatic. The join link
dies at window close, so nobody new gets in, but a student already in the server
stays there until Alex removes them. See *Access model* for why that is acceptable
here and exactly what it costs.

## Constraints

- **No hard deadline.** This was the most architecture-shaping constraint in the
  original plan — 45-55 working hours against the 2026-11-16 screening — and as of
  2026-11-13 it is gone. Four of the seven planned days had already elapsed when Alex
  decided the project would not lead the screening, and rather than compress a
  56-hour plan into three days the work is now ordered by dependency (see *Build
  order*). The remaining estimate is roughly **49 hours**, stated as information
  rather than as a budget. Nothing in this design is justified by the clock any more;
  everything that was has been removed.
- **Stack is partly prescribed** by the evaluator: Go, TypeScript, React/Next.js,
  PostgreSQL, AWS (S3, SQS, ECS), Docker.
- **Alex is basically new to Go.** This is the constraint that shaped the
  architecture more than any other.
- **Dual delivery:** the repository must stand alone for a reader who never runs
  it, *and* a live link must work.
- **One rehearsal before any real student.** The feature freeze is gone with the
  deadline, but the thing the freeze existed to protect is not: the whole path must be
  exercised once, end to end, by a real second person on their own device and their
  own Discord account, before live mode is enabled. That is a gate on *shipping*, not
  on a date.
- **A dedicated Discord server, not Alex's community server.** This became a hard
  requirement with the invite-only access model, for the reason given in *Access
  model*: once a student is in the server, `@everyone` is all that protects every
  other channel in it.

### Verified platform constraints

These were confirmed by research during the session and are load-bearing:

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Riot Sign On (RSO) OAuth requires an **already-approved production** API key; dev keys do not qualify, and review runs weeks to months | "Log in with Riot ID" is **not buildable** on any near-term timeline |
| Riot `by-riot-id` → PUUID lookup **works on a development key**; RSO only governs `/accounts/me` | Match-data ingestion is viable without RSO |
| Riot **development keys expire every 24 hours** | Ingestion must degrade gracefully and stay off the money path |
| Discord invite `max_age` is a **countdown from creation**, capped at 604800s (7 days). There is no settable expiry timestamp; the `expires_at` in the response is derived from `created_at + max_age` and is read-only | An invite **cannot be minted in advance to become valid later**. It must be created *at* window open, which is why a scheduled worker survives the simplification |
| A Discord invite gates **joining the guild, not remaining in it** | Invite expiry removes nobody. This is the whole reason access-ending is manual in this design |
| Removing a role does **not** disconnect a member already in a voice channel; "Disconnect Members when Roles Removed" is still an open Discord feature request | Confirmed by research 2026-11-13. No longer load-bearing here, but it is why the superseded role design needed a separate force-disconnect call |
| Invite `temporary: true` ("grant temporary membership") removes the member when they disconnect from voice, unless a role was assigned. Community-documented, not in the official reference | **Deliberately unused.** A dropped connection mid-session would eject a paying student whose single-use invite is already spent |
| `DELETE /invites/{code}` needs **Manage Channels** on the channel (or Manage Guild); `GET /channels/{id}/invites` the same | The bot needs Create Instant Invite + Manage Channels, and nothing else |
| AWS SQS `DelaySeconds` caps at **15 minutes** | SQS cannot schedule anything for a session days out; the Postgres due-work table does the scheduling |
| Discord member verification / rules screening leaves a joining member `pending` | A `pending` member cannot connect to voice, which looks exactly like the access system failing. Disable screening on the coaching server or handle `pending` explicitly |
| Stripe Checkout `expires_at` has a **30-minute minimum**, measured from session creation | The slot hold is 30 minutes and is created in the same transaction as the Checkout session |

## Premises

1. **Dual audience.** Corgi's engineers may read it; real students will use it once
   live mode is enabled. "Usable by a student" means the student-facing path is
   complete and exercised end to end by a real second person on a test card **before
   live mode is switched on** — a gate on shipping, no longer on a screening date.
   The live-money switch is a deliberate config change (see Payment mode).
   *(Corrected by Alex during the session — the original premise treated it as a
   demo only.)*
2. **Riot ID login is out; Discord OAuth replaces it.** Discord OAuth identifies the
   student and yields the email address the access notification needs. It no longer
   yields a `guilds.join` token, because the student now joins by clicking a link.
   Riot ID becomes a profile field used for match lookup, not authentication.
3. **The paid, time-windowed access lifecycle is the centerpiece.** Premium before
   coverage attaches, an effective window, revocation on expiry or refund. Depth here
   beats breadth anywhere else. **Amended 2026-11-13:** revocation now means the
   credential is destroyed and a removal obligation is recorded, not that the platform
   ejects the member. That is a weaker guarantee and a more honest one, and it is
   still the same lifecycle.
4. **TypeScript owns the money path; Go owns ingestion.** A Go component is
   **mandatory for the submission**: it was exempt from every cut rank when a cut
   list existed, and it survives the list's removal unchanged. *(Revised during the
   session — see Cross-Model Perspective.)*
5. **Both deliverables required:** a standalone README plus architecture docs, and
   a working deploy.
6. **Two-way calendar sync and a designed marketing page are the lowest-signal
   items.** Cut to an `.ics` download and a clean modest page, roughly 1.5 hours
   total. With the deadline gone there is no gate on the styling half; it simply
   lands after the functional page.
7. **Access ending is a tracked manual obligation, not an automated action.**
   *(Alex, 2026-11-13: "I'm ok with having to manually remove users since in my own
   workflow, once a student is done, I'm likely going to remove them from the server
   anyways.")* The platform's job is to make that obligation visible, dated and
   auditable.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Entitlement engine, depth-first (minimal viable)
Next.js + TypeScript with a separate worker entrypoint in the same image. Discord
OAuth, DB-enforced booking holds, Stripe Checkout with idempotent webhooks,
Postgres due-work reconciler, `/ops` page, S3 presigned VOD upload. Docker +
compose committed, deployed on a PaaS. Risk Low, Completeness 7/10. AWS surface
limited to S3, and **no Go at all**.

### Approach B: AWS-forward on ECS (ideal architecture)
Identical application code on ECS Fargate behind an ALB, reconciler as a second
service, ECR + CI. Completeness 9/10. **Rejected:** ECS + IAM + ALB + ECR for a
first-timer is the likeliest single cause of shipping a dead link, and a partial ECS
setup reads worse than a documented deferral. The deadline's removal does not revive
this: the reason was never only the hours.

### Approach C: A, plus Go ingestion in the core (selected)
Approach A built in A's order, with a minimum Go ingestion service promoted **into
the non-negotiable core** so that Go cannot vanish from the submission. The
charge-file reconciler that was originally a second add-on is **out of the plan** —
not for want of hours, which are no longer scarce, but because it belongs after a
working loop. Recorded as the first post-launch extension.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C.** Every item below is committed. The cut list that used to rank them
was a deadline instrument and is gone; what remains is the *Build order*, which says
when each piece is built rather than which gets dropped. Two items were never
cuttable at any point and still are not: the payment-to-entitlement path and the Go

### Identity and payment

- **Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript**; PostgreSQL with migrations; a second
  entrypoint in the same image runs the worker. Docker + `docker-compose.yml`
  committed so the whole system runs locally with one command.
- **Discord OAuth** (`identify`, **`email`**) is the only login. `guilds.join` is
  **no longer requested**: the student joins by clicking an invite, so the app never
  acts on their behalf against Discord, and the entire stored-token and
  refresh-rotation protocol the original design needed is gone with it. The `email`
  scope is still required and not optional — `identify` alone does not return an
  email address, and five committed behaviors depend on having one: the confirmation
  email with `.ics`, the pre-session reminder, **the access email at window open**,
  the expired-hold-race notification, and the Alex-cancellation notice. Capturing it
  at login rather than from Stripe means it is available *before* payment, which the
  expired-hold notification needs.
- **Payment mode (decided):** the deployment runs **Stripe test mode** until the
  rehearsal has passed. That rehearsal is performed by a real second person
  on their own device and their own Discord account using a Stripe **test card** —
  so the unattended path is genuinely exercised without moving real money through a
  seven-day-old public app. Live cutover after the screening is a config change:
  swap keys, create live Price objects, re-verify the webhook signing secret, run
  one real transaction, then enable public booking. The README states this plainly.
- **Three SKUs** as Stripe Price objects created in Phase 4, USD, amounts fixed:

  | SKU | Price | Duration | Effective rate |
  |---|---:|---|---:|
  | VOD Review | **$30** | 1 h | $30/h |
  | Live Coaching | **$40** | 1 h | $40/h |
  | Both | **$60** | 2 h | $30/h |

  Stripe amounts are integer cents: `3000`, `4000`, `6000`. Note for Alex rather
  than a problem: Both is a **$10 bundle discount** against buying the two
  separately ($70), which prices the live hour inside the bundle at the VOD rate.
  Deliberate-looking and fine; just know that is what the numbers say.

### SKU semantics

All three SKUs are **scheduled, synchronous sessions**. They differ only in duration
and in whether a replay upload is expected beforehand. Everything else — slot,
window, Discord access, refund policy, fulfillment — is identical.

| SKU | Duration | Consumes a slot? | Window | Discord access | `.rofl` upload | Fulfilled when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Live Coaching** | 1 h | Yes | = the booking | Invite at window open | No | Window closes |
| **VOD Review** | 1 h | Yes | = the booking | Invite at window open | Expected | Window closes |
| **Both** | 2 h, contiguous | Yes | = the booking | Invite at window open | Expected | Window closes |

**The upload is not a precondition.** If the replay has not arrived by session
start, the session runs anyway and part of it is spent helping the student locate
and send the file — Alex's stated intent, and the reason this design has no
missing-upload fallback branch, no deadline check, and no automatic refund. A
reminder at T-24 h asks for the file in advance so the full hour goes to the review,
but nothing is gated on it.

Consequently there is **one refund policy for all three SKUs** (see Cancellation),
one fulfillment rule (the window closed), and no review state machine.

**`.rofl` only.** The upload accepts League replay files and nothing else: roughly
**10-40 MB** for a full game, one `PUT` to a presigned S3 URL, no multipart (S3
accepts single PUTs to 5 GB), no video transcoding. At that size a minimal
spinner is enough and no progress bar is built, but the figure is stated so the
choice is checkable rather than assumed. The `upload_grant` row authorizes PUTs from payment until **session end
plus one hour**, because students may well upload during the session itself. Known
limitation worth stating in the README: a `.rofl` only opens in a League client on a
matching patch, so a sufficiently old replay may be unplayable — a domain
constraint, not a system failure.

### Booking, holds, and overlap

- Availability is stored as **continuous open ranges** (a recurring rule in Alex's
  timezone), **edited by direct SQL or a seed row for this release** — no admin UI
  (Open Question 4). Continuous ranges rather than pre-cut one-hour slots, because
  the Both SKU is a contiguous 2-hour booking and cannot be assembled from fixed
  1-hour cells.
- **SKU selection comes before the calendar.** The picker cannot search availability
  until it knows whether it needs a 1-hour or a 2-hour contiguous opening, so the
  flow is: choose SKU → choose time → pay.
- **Empty-day messaging for the Both SKU (decided).** When a day holds open time but
  no contiguous 2-hour block, the day renders **empty with an explicit per-day note:
  "No continuous 2-hour blocks on this day."** Per-day, not a global banner, so the
  student can see at a glance which days are worth checking. Without it a fragmented
  calendar is indistinguishable from a broken one — and this is the only place in the
  product where "no availability" has a cause the student would otherwise have to
  guess at.
- A **15-minute gap** is reserved after every booking, enforced by the database (see
  below), so two windows can never be open at once and a departing student cannot
  overlap the next session even with the revoke lagging a full poll interval.
- **Availability search applies the same padding as the constraint, on both sides.**
  A start time is offered only if `[start, end + 15 min)` overlaps no existing
  `pending_payment`/`paid`/`active` booking's reserved range. Without this the
  picker would cheerfully offer 3:05 after a 2:00-3:00 booking and the insert would
  then be refused by the database. The Both SKU's "continuous 2-hour block" test
  includes the trailing gap, so the committed empty-day note is accurate rather than
  optimistic.
- **A constraint rejection is not a concurrency loss, and says so.** "That slot was
  just taken" is reserved for a genuine race. A rejection the search should have
  prevented shows "That time is no longer available — the calendar has been
  refreshed", reloads availability, and is logged as a search/constraint
  disagreement, because recurring instances mean the search is wrong.
- **Minimum lead time: 45 minutes, enforced twice.** The picker offers only future
  occurrences and nothing starting sooner than 45 minutes from now — and the floor is
  **re-checked inside the transaction that creates the hold and the Checkout
  session**, measured from that moment, not from when the page was rendered. Search
  alone is not enough: a picker left open for twenty minutes would otherwise walk a
  stale slot straight past the floor. A stale start reuses the "That time is no
  longer available — the calendar has been refreshed" path. Measuring the floor from
  Checkout-session creation is what keeps it strictly longer than the session's own
  30-minute life. This is
  load-bearing, not politeness: the Checkout session lives 30 minutes, so a slot
  starting in 5 minutes could be paid for after its own window had already opened —
  leaving a student who paid for a session that is partly or wholly in the past, and
  non-refundable on creation under the 24-hour rule. Bookings cannot be made inside
  the window, so `expired_unfulfilled` always means a platform failure and its
  automatic refund stays correct.
- **A lapsed hold is swept by the poller.** The exclusion constraint keys on
  `state`, not on a timestamp, so an abandoned Checkout would block its slot *and*
  its gap forever until something writes the terminal state. At hold creation a
  due-work row is enqueued for the hold's expiry instant; the same ~30-second poller
  claims it with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and transitions the booking to
  `expired_hold`. The slot is not re-offered until that transition lands.
- **The hold is created when the Stripe Checkout session is created, not at slot
  selection.** Stripe measures its 30-minute minimum `expires_at` from *session
  creation*; a hold started earlier at slot selection would always need an
  `expires_at` less than 30 minutes away and Stripe would reject it. So: picking a
  slot in the UI reserves nothing, clicking through to pay creates the hold and the
  Checkout session in one transaction, and the hold is set to expire **60 seconds
  after** the Checkout session. Stripe therefore refuses payment before the slot is
  released, which closes the *authorization* race — a new payment cannot start once
  the slot is free. It does **not** close webhook-delivery lag: a payment completed
  at minute 29 can deliver after minute 31, which is exactly the deliberate remainder
  the Expired-hold race handler below exists to absorb. Two students on
  the picker at once is resolved first-click-wins, and the loser is told the slot
  was just taken.
- Overlap and double-booking are prevented **in the database**, by a Postgres
  exclusion constraint:

  ```sql
  -- A stored, trigger-maintained range. NOT a generated column and NOT an
  -- inline expression: see the note below.
  ALTER TABLE bookings ADD COLUMN reserved tstzrange;

  CREATE FUNCTION bookings_set_reserved() RETURNS trigger AS $$
  BEGIN
    NEW.reserved := CASE WHEN NEW.starts_at IS NULL THEN NULL
      ELSE tstzrange(NEW.starts_at, NEW.ends_at + interval '15 minutes') END;
    RETURN NEW;
  END $$ LANGUAGE plpgsql;

  CREATE TRIGGER bookings_reserved BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE ON bookings
    FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION bookings_set_reserved();

  ALTER TABLE bookings ADD CONSTRAINT bookings_no_overlap
    EXCLUDE USING gist (reserved WITH &&)
    WHERE (reserved IS NOT NULL
           AND state IN ('pending_payment','paid','active'));
  ```

  **Why a trigger and not the obvious inline expression.** The natural form,
  `EXCLUDE USING gist (tstzrange(starts_at, ends_at + interval '15 minutes') WITH &&)`,
  **does not compile.** An `EXCLUDE` constraint builds an index, PostgreSQL requires
  index expressions to be `IMMUTABLE`, and `timestamptz + interval` is only `STABLE`
  — adding `'1 day'` and `'24 hours'` to a timestamptz give different answers across
  a DST boundary, so the operator's volatility is per-function, not per-argument. The
  migration would fail with *"functions in index expression must be marked
  IMMUTABLE."* Wrapping the arithmetic in a hand-declared `IMMUTABLE` function would
  work and is a known trick — safe here only because a fixed 15-minute interval has
  no day or month component — but lying to the planner about volatility can silently
  corrupt an index, and it is a bad thing to have to defend in an interview. The
  trigger keeps the derivation in the database, where a buggy application write
  cannot bypass it, and indexes a plain stored column.

  **The gap lives inside the reserved range, so the database enforces it.** Book
  2:00-3:00 and Postgres reserves 2:00-3:15; nothing can start before 3:15. The gap
  — not the overlap check — is what keeps a departing student out of the next session
  while revoke lags up to a poll interval, and that guarantee should not depend on
  slot-generation code being correct. One constraint covers duplicate bookings,
  overlapping windows, variable durations, and the gap.

  `reserved IS NOT NULL` is defensive rather than load-bearing now that every SKU is
  a scheduled session: `tstzrange(NULL, NULL)` is *not* null but the unbounded range
  `(,)`, which overlaps everything, so a future windowless product would otherwise
  block the whole table.
- **Timezones:** every window stored as a UTC instant, availability stored as a rule
  in Alex's timezone, rendered in the viewer's, both shown on confirmation.

### Booking and entitlement states

One enumerated set, referenced by the exclusion constraint predicate, the
cancellation table, the reconciler's notion of an open window, and the README
diagram.

**Booking states:** `pending_payment` → `paid` → `active` → `completed`. Terminal
branches: `expired_hold` (hold lapsed unpaid, written by the poller),
`payment_orphaned` (paid but the slot was lost), `cancelled_refunded`,
`cancelled_no_refund`, `refund_failed` (refund retries exhausted; needs a human).
There is no separate `held` state — the hold and the Checkout session are created in
one transaction, so the row is never observable without a session attached, and
`pending_payment` is the initial state.

**Entitlement states:** `pending` (created, window not yet open) → `open` (window
current, role should be held) → `closed` (window ended normally). Terminal
branches: `voided` (never opened), `revoked` (cut short by refund or late
cancellation), `expired_unfulfilled` (window passed without the student ever
getting in).

**Supporting states:** `upload_grant` `open` | `closed`; `access_events` is
append-only. There are no review states, because fulfillment is window closure for
every SKU. The OAuth-token state machine (`live` | `dead`) is **gone** — no stored
user token is needed once `guilds.join` is dropped.

**Invite states** (one row per entitlement, in `access_invites`): `issued` (code live
on the channel) → `consumed` (the student joined) → `deleted` (code removed at window
close or on revocation). Terminal branch: `unused_expired` — the code was live for the
whole window and never used, which is a **no-show** and carries no refund.

**`expired_unfulfilled` has a sharper definition than it did.** It means the window
closed and **no working code was ever issued**, because invite creation failed every
retry. That is a platform failure and keeps its automatic full refund. An
issued-but-unclicked code is a no-show, not a failure, and the two are told apart by
whether an `access_invites` row ever reached `issued` — a distinction the role design
could not draw cleanly, and one of two places where this change improved precision
rather than only removing scope.

**Removal-obligation states** (one row per member who joined, in `member_cleanup`):
`queued` (entitlement closed, student still in the guild) → `done` (Alex confirms
removal in `/ops`) or `auto_resolved` (a reconcile pass finds the member already
gone). This table is the honest record of the one thing the platform does not do for
itself.

**Which definition of "open" is authoritative:** the **stored state** governs the
reconciler's diff; the **timestamps** govern the transition that sets it. The worker
moves an entitlement `pending → open` at window start and `open → closed` at window
end, and the diff keeps a live invite only for entitlements in state `open`. This
matters in two cases the design depends on: a window that has started but whose row
the worker has not yet flipped issues nothing (correct — never early), and a refund
inside a current window sets `revoked`, which deletes the code immediately even though
the clock still says the window is open.

### Payment-to-entitlement

- **Stripe Checkout (hosted)**, `expires_at` = creation + 30 minutes, with the hold
  expiring 60 seconds later, so no card data ever touches the app.
- The webhook handler is the **only** writer that creates an entitlement:
  signature-verified, idempotent on `event.id` via a `processed_events` table,
  inside one transaction with the booking state transition.
- **Expired-hold race:** if a webhook arrives for a booking whose hold lapsed, the
  handler re-checks the slot. Still free → reseat and proceed. Taken → **immediate
  full refund**, booking → `payment_orphaned`, student emailed, row raised red in
  `/ops`. This is a system fault, so the >24h/<24h policy does not apply; it is
  always a full refund.
- **Checkout return page:** shows "confirming payment" and polls booking state. If
  the entitlement does not exist after 60 seconds it tells the student the
  confirmation email is authoritative and stops spinning.

### Access model: a single-use invite, minted at window open

**The primitive.** At window open the worker creates one invite on the coaching voice
channel:

```
POST /channels/{channel_id}/invites
{ "max_age": <seconds remaining in the window>,
  "max_uses": 1,
  "unique":   true,
  "temporary": false }
```

The returned code and its derived `expires_at` are stored against the entitlement in
`access_invites`. At window close, or the instant an entitlement goes `revoked`, the
worker calls `DELETE /invites/{code}`. Both calls are idempotent in effect: a stored
code prevents re-creation, and deleting an already-gone code returns 404, which the
worker treats as success.

**Why this still needs a scheduler.** `max_age` counts down from *creation*, so an
invite cannot be minted in advance to become valid at 2 PM. It has to be created at
2 PM. The due-work poller built for the role design is therefore unchanged — the
simplification removed Discord mechanics, not timing machinery.

**Why `max_age` is the whole remaining window and not 15 minutes.** Alex's initial
framing was a 2:00-2:15 join window. Minting at window open already delivers the
guarantee that window was protecting: before 2:00 no code exists, so there is nothing
to join with, and `max_uses: 1` caps a leaked link at one join. A 15-minute ceiling
buys nothing further and costs something real — a student twenty minutes late is
locked out of a session they paid for, which the platform would then have to refund
or argue about. **Decided by Alex on 2026-11-13: the link lives as long as the
session.** For a 2:00-3:00 booking the code is created at 2:00 with
`max_age: 3600`; for a 2-hour Both booking, `max_age: 7200`. Not a tunable —
`max_age` is computed from the booking's own `ends_at`, so a reprice, a different SKU
duration or a future 90-minute session needs no constant changed and cannot drift out
of step with the window the student paid for.

**How the student gets the link.** Two surfaces, in priority order:

1. **The booking page**, authenticated by their Discord login, which is where the
   confirmation email and the `.ics` both point. It polls and reveals the join button
   the moment the code exists, showing a countdown before that. Primary surface
   because it has no delivery latency inside a window measured in minutes.
2. **An access email at window open**, best-effort. If it lands late, the booking page
   has already worked.

If the student opens neither and the window closes with the code unused, that is a
**no-show**: booking → `completed`, invite → `unused_expired`, no refund.
`expired_unfulfilled` and its automatic refund are reserved for the narrower case
where the platform never managed to issue a working code at all.

**What this guarantees, stated precisely:**

- **Nobody can join before the window opens.** No code exists to join with. This was
  Alex's stated core requirement and it is fully enforced.
- **Nobody new can join after the window closes.** The code is deleted, and it was
  single-use regardless.
- **A refund or late cancellation inside an open window kills the credential** within
  one poll interval.
- **A student already inside the server is not removed.** Not at window close, not
  ever, until Alex removes them.

**The exposure, named rather than buried.** Without a bot-managed role there is no
per-student channel permission, so the coaching voice channel must be open to
`@everyone` in the server and **the server itself is the access boundary**. Two
consequences:

1. **This requires a dedicated coaching server.** On Alex's community server every
   paying student would acquire permanent `@everyone` access to every channel in it.
   On a dedicated server whose only content is the coaching channels, the blast radius
   is the coaching channels.
2. **A student not yet removed can wander into a later session.** Bounded rather than
   eliminated: the exclusion constraint and the 15-minute gap guarantee windows never
   overlap, so this needs a stale member *and* Alex not noticing someone in a voice
   channel Alex is sitting in. The `member_cleanup` queue exists to make staleness
   visible instead of silent.

This is a real downgrade from the role design, and the README says so in those words.
It is accepted because it matches what Alex already does — *"once a student is done,
I'm likely going to remove them from the server anyways"* — and because a tracked
manual step is more honest than an automated one nobody has tested.

**The named upgrade path**, for when the core loop is done: a **per-member channel
permission overwrite**, `PUT /channels/{channel_id}/permissions/{user_id}` with
`type: 1`, allowing `VIEW_CHANNEL` + `CONNECT` for that one member while `@everyone`
is denied. It needs Manage Permissions on the channel, involves no role hierarchy and
no kicking, and restores per-student channel-level control with the server still open.
Pair it with a force-disconnect (`PATCH /guilds/{g}/members/{u}` with
`channel_id: null`, needs Move Members) only if ejecting a connected member turns out
to matter. Recorded as the first post-launch extension to the access model.

### Reconciler, not scheduler

The reconciliation loop is intact and **narrower** than the role version, which is the
one unambiguous win of this change.

- A Postgres due-work table polled every ~30 seconds with
  `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`.
- Each pass computes desired state from entitlements and compares it against actual
  state fetched from `GET /channels/{channel_id}/invites`:
  - **Desired:** every entitlement in state `open` has exactly one live invite code.
  - **Actual:** the codes Discord reports for the coaching channel.
  - **Diff:** no code for an open entitlement → create one. A live code belonging to
    an entitlement that is `closed`, `revoked` or `voided` → delete it. A code that
    vanished while its window is still open and unconsumed → recreate it.
- Every action and every failure appends to `access_events`; 429 responses back off.

**Bounded authority, and this time trivially so.** The reconciler touches **only
invite codes recorded in its own `access_invites` table**. An invite created by hand in
the Discord UI is reported by the API, logged once as unmanaged, and **left alone**. It
changes no roles, no member attributes and no channel settings, and it removes no
members. The unbounded-destructive-diff hazard the review cycle caught in the role
design — where removals had to apply to every holder of `coaching-active`, including
members the system had no record of — cannot arise, and
`RECONCILER_EXEMPT_DISCORD_IDS` is deleted along with it.

**Where the reconciler stops and a human starts.** When an entitlement reaches
`closed` and its invite was consumed, the pass writes a `member_cleanup` row in state
`queued` and does nothing else. A later pass that finds the member no longer in the
guild flips it to `auto_resolved`. Alex clears the rest from `/ops`. Desired state is
recorded, divergence is detected and dated, and the action belongs to a person — a
defensible shape for a system whose last mile is outside its authority, and the part
worth describing in the README rather than hiding.

### Cancellation, refunds, and no-shows

Separate concerns, each with defined behavior:

| Event | Slot | Money | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student cancels **>24h** before | Released | Full refund | Entitlement voided before it ever opens; no code is ever minted |
| Student cancels **<24h** before (before window start) | Released | **No refund** | Entitlement `voided` before it opens, so no code is ever minted |
| **Alex cancels, before window start** (`/ops` cancel booking) | Released | Full refund, enqueued by the app after the slot is released | Entitlement `voided`; student emailed |
| **Alex refunds at or after window start** (`/ops` refund booking) | Consumed | Full refund, enqueued | Open window → entitlement `revoked`, code deleted on the next pass, `member_cleanup` queued if the student had joined; closed window → terminal entitlement untouched |
| **No-show** (window elapses unused) | Consumed | No refund | A working code was issued and available; booking → `completed`, invite → `unused_expired` |
| **Refund during an open window** (Alex-initiated or dispute) | Consumed | Refund per Stripe | Entitlement → `revoked`; the next pass deletes the code within one poll interval and queues `member_cleanup` if the student joined; booking page shows a banner; student emailed. **A student already in voice is not ejected** — Alex does that by hand |

**How the system tells an Alex-cancellation from a refund-during-window.** These two
rows prescribe opposite slot outcomes and a bare Stripe refund webhook carries no
intent, so the signal is stated rather than inferred:

1. **A refund webhook matched to any preceding in-app record — a cancellation *or* a
   refund — takes no further action.** Both `/ops` controls write their record first
   and enqueue the Stripe call second, so the webhook always arrives after the record
   exists. The record, not the webhook, already decided the slot and the entitlement:
   cancel booking released the slot and `voided` the entitlement; refund booking
   consumed the slot and set `revoked` for an open window or left a closed one
   untouched.
2. **A refund arriving with no preceding in-app cancel or refund record** — issued
   straight from the Stripe dashboard, or a dispute — falls back to timing:
   **before** window start is treated as a cancellation (slot released, entitlement
   `voided`); **at or after** window start is treated as refund-during-window (slot
   consumed, entitlement `revoked`, access removed on the next pass).

The visible policy line, identical for all three SKUs: **full refund more than 24
hours before the session, no refund inside 24 hours, and a full refund any time the
platform fails to deliver access.** No SKU-specific carve-outs, because all three are
scheduled sessions.

### Operations and notifications

- **`/ops` page, really authenticated.** Discord OAuth plus an owner-ID allowlist
  (`OPS_OWNER_DISCORD_IDS`); the previously planned env var remains **only** as an
  additional kill switch, because an env var controls whether a route exists, not
  who may reach it. The page shows booking state, window, last reconcile timestamp,
  a red list of failed actions with retry — covering reconciler actions and failed
  refunds alike — and **the outstanding `member_cleanup` queue**, which is the one
  place the system asks a human to finish something it started. It carries three
  operator controls:

  - **Cancel booking** — releases the slot, voids the entitlement, records the
    cancellation, then enqueues the refund. Available only before window start.
  - **Refund booking** — for a window that is already open or has closed, where
    there is no slot to release. Its effect on the entitlement depends on which:
    **used during an open window** it sets the entitlement to `revoked`, so the next
    reconcile pass deletes the invite code and queues `member_cleanup` if the student
    had already joined (this is the behavior mandatory test 5 asserts); **used after
    window close** it leaves the terminal
    entitlement untouched and moves only money. Either way it records the refund
    against the booking first, so the resulting Stripe webhook matches a known record
    and disambiguation rule 1 applies — rule 2's timing fallback is only ever reached
    by a refund issued outside the app. This control exists because the design
    commits to a refund in exactly that situation: an `expired_unfulfilled`
    entitlement, where the student never got in and the failure was ours.

  - **Confirm removal** — marks a `member_cleanup` row `done` after Alex has removed
    the member in the Discord app. It performs no Discord call, which is the whole
    point: the platform records the obligation and its discharge, and never acts on a
    member. A row that a later reconcile pass finds already gone from the guild flips
    to `auto_resolved` without Alex touching it, so the queue reflects reality rather
    than bookkeeping.

  No review-marking controls exist, because fulfillment is window closure for every
  SKU. The page displays a student's Discord username and the Stripe payment-intent
  ID only — never card data, which never reaches the app, and never an email address.

- **Refunds are durable work, not a fire-and-forget API call.** The cancel path
  records the cancellation with a `refund_pending` marker and enqueues the Stripe
  refund as a due-work row; the poller retries it with backoff. Sequencing the refund
  last means a failed call would otherwise leave a student with no session, no
  entitlement and no money — the single worst state this system can produce. A
  refund that exhausts its retries lands in state `refund_failed` and sits red in the
  `/ops` list until a human clears it.

- **Students cancel from the booking page**, which is authenticated by their Discord
  login and linked from the confirmation email. The control disappears at window
  start: once the session is open, the policy outcome is identical to a no-show and
  there is nothing useful left to cancel.
- **Confirmation email** with `.ics` attached, plus a T-24 h reminder that also asks
  VOD and Both students to send their `.rofl` ahead of time so the full session goes
  to the review. **For a booking made less than 24 hours out**, the T-24 h trigger is
  already past: the file request is folded into the confirmation email and the
  separate reminder is suppressed. The visible policy states that such a booking is
  non-refundable from the moment it is made. Mandatory because real students will pay once live mode is enabled,
  and silence after payment reads as failure — exactly the manual-DM loop this
  platform exists to remove.
- **Access email at window open**, best-effort and secondary to the booking page,
  carrying the single-use join link and its expiry time in the student's timezone.
- **S3 presigned upload** for `.rofl` files, authorized by an `upload_grant` row
  valid from payment until session end plus one hour.

### Minimum Go deliverable (mandatory, no cut rank)

A standalone Go binary — not a service, no HTTP server: take a Riot ID, resolve it
to a PUUID, fetch the last 20 match IDs and summaries, upsert them into Postgres.
Rate-limited, retrying, idempotent on match ID, and tolerant of the 24-hour dev-key
expiry — on auth failure it logs and exits without affecting anything else. **No
prep-sheet UI** in this release; the rows in Postgres are the deliverable.

It **was exempt from every rank on the cut list** for as long as that list existed:
Premise 4 calls a Go component mandatory, and a mandatory item that is cuttable is not
mandatory — the unresolved contradiction the reviewer carried across both rounds. With
the list gone the point is simply that Go ships. It is scheduled
late (Phase 9) on purpose: it is standalone and cannot break the money path, so it is
the safest thing to land after the loop works, and that is a deliberate risk-ordering
choice rather than an afterthought. It is also the one phase with no predecessor but
the schema, so it can move earlier freely.

### Why this ordering

The money path stays in TypeScript because Alex is new to Go and that path is where
silent idempotency and ordering bugs live. Go lands where failure is harmless and
retryable. The access model is convergent state rather than fired events, which is
what makes the reconciliation loop — and therefore the whole Beagle analogy —
possible. That survived the 2026-11-13 simplification intact: invite codes are state
to be diffed exactly as roles were, and the loop lost its destructive reach over
members without losing its shape. What it gained is a boundary it can state in one
sentence — it touches only codes it issued — and a last mile it hands to a person
with a dated record, which is a more defensible system than one that ejects people
using a Discord behavior nobody has tested.

## Build order (dependency-ordered, ~49 hours)

No dates. The original seven-day hour-indexed table, its two failure-triggered gates,
its noon freeze and its five-item cut list were all artifacts of a deadline that no
longer applies, and keeping them would mean pretending the plan still has a shape it
does not. What replaces them is an order: each phase depends on the one before it, and
nothing is scheduled against a clock. The hour figures are estimates for sequencing,
not a budget to defend.

The Discord simplification took the access work from **14.5 hours to 7.5**:

| Item | Role design | Invite design |
|---|---:|---:|
| Discord app, bot, channel setup | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| Discord OAuth (was + `guilds.join` + token rotation) | 3.0 | 1.0 |
| Reconciler core and diff | 5.0 | 3.0 |
| Revoke (was role removal + force-disconnect + 403 handling) | 2.0 | 0.5 |
| Invite fallback path | 1.0 | 0 (it is the primary path now) |
| `member_cleanup` queue and its `/ops` surface | 0 | 0.5 |
| Access-related mandatory tests | 3.0 | 2.0 |
| **Total** | **14.5** | **7.5** |

**Phase 0 — external lead time, started first because waiting is the cost.**
Transactional email provider with sender-domain verification (DNS propagation runs
hours to days). Riot production key application. `git init` plus a GitHub repo and CI
skeleton — the directory is still not under version control, so nothing is
checkpointed. **The invite probe** (see The Assignment). ~3 h of work, after which the
waiting proceeds in the background.

**Phase 1 — the thing that cannot be retrofitted.** State machine, schema, migrations,
the `reserved` trigger and the exclusion constraint. Everything downstream assumes the
database refuses overlaps, so this comes first and is tested before anything is built
on it. ~4 h.

**Phase 2 — deploy before features.** PaaS project, managed Postgres, secret wiring,
and a first migration run against the hosted database. The exclusion constraint needs
gist support on the managed instance, so this is where that gets discovered rather than
much later. A design that cannot be deployed is worth less than one feature short.
~1 h.

**Phase 3 — booking without money.** Availability as continuous ranges, gap-aware
duration search, the 45-minute lead time enforced twice, holds, the hold-expiry
sweeper, and a functional unstyled landing page. Bookable, not payable. ~7 h.

**Phase 4 — money.** Stripe account, three Price objects, hosted Checkout, the webhook
with signature verification and `event.id` idempotency, the transactional
payment-to-entitlement write, and the expired-hold race. This is where silent bugs cost
real money, so it is built when nothing else is in flight. ~5.5 h.

**Phase 5 — access.** Discord application and bot with Create Instant Invite and Manage
Channels, Discord OAuth, the reconciler, invite issue and delete, `access_events`, and
the `member_cleanup` queue. ~5 h. *(Phase 4 before Phase 5 deliberately: an entitlement
that does not exist cannot be reconciled.)*

**Phase 6 — the edges that make it a product.** Cancellation and refunds across all
branches with durable retry, the student cancel control, `/ops` behind Discord OAuth
with its three controls, the confirmation email with `.ics`, the T-24 h reminder
including the short-horizon rule, and the access email. ~10 h.

**Phase 7 — the eight mandatory tests** as an explicit pass, with Discord and Stripe
doubles and a real Postgres service container for tests 3 and 8. ~2 h on top of the
tests written alongside Phases 1-6.

**Phase 8 — AWS.** S3 bucket, IAM user scoped to it, CORS for browser presigned PUTs,
and the `.rofl` upload path. ~1 h. Last because nothing else depends on it, and first
to go if the upload turns out not to be worth it.

**Phase 9 — Go ingestion.** The standalone binary. ~4 h. Independent of everything
above, so it can land any time after Phase 1 gives it a database. It is listed late
because it cannot break the money path and therefore never needs to be ahead of it.

**Phase 10 — the gate that survived the deadline.** One full rehearsal by a real
second person, on their own device and their own Discord account, with a Stripe test
card. Then the fallout fixes it exposes, the README with its failure-modes table
written from *observed* behavior, both architecture diagrams, deploy hardening, and
landing-page styling. **Live mode is enabled only after the rehearsal passes.** ~6 h.

**Deliberately not in the plan:** the charge-file reconciler (first post-launch
extension), the Riot prep-sheet UI, two-way calendar sync, an availability admin UI,
and the per-member channel-overwrite upgrade to the access model. Each is recorded
where it belongs rather than carried as scope.

## Mandatory tests (the floor; CI runs all eight on push)

**Test strategy, because five of these touch third parties.** Discord is a **fake
client** implementing the three calls used (create invite, list channel invites,
delete invite) over an in-memory channel model — down from four, and none of them
mutate a member, which is why the fake is now small enough to trust; Stripe webhooks
are **replayed fixtures** posted to the real handler with real signature verification
against a test secret. Tests 3 and 8 run against a **real PostgreSQL** service container,
because they assert database behavior and a double would prove nothing. No test needs
live Discord or Stripe credentials, so the whole suite runs on push. A manual
smoke script exercises the real APIs once per deploy, separately from CI.
**Time is a double too:** due-at timestamps and window boundaries are seeded relative
to the test clock and the poller is invoked directly for a single pass, so no test
waits out a 31-minute hold expiry or a 60-second poll interval.

1. **Hold expiry transitions and releases.** After the hold's expiry instant the
   poller writes `expired_hold`, and a second booking for the same range then
   succeeds. Asserted as a state transition and an ordering — not as Stripe's live
   refusal, which cannot occur inside a push build given the 30-minute floor.
2. A replayed Stripe webhook creates exactly one entitlement.
3. Two simultaneous bookings for the same time: one succeeds, one is rejected by the
   exclusion constraint. A 2-hour Both booking is rejected when it would span an
   existing 1-hour booking, and accepted into a clear 2-hour-plus-gap opening.
4. **Boundary behavior as inequalities, not instants:** no invite code exists before
   window start; a code is observed within 60 s after window start; the code is gone
   within 60 s after window end. The first assertion is the core product guarantee and
   is the one test that must never be allowed to go soft.
5. A refund during an open window sets `revoked`, the invite code is deleted within
   one poll interval even though the clock still says the window is open, and a
   `member_cleanup` row is queued if that code had been consumed.
6. **The reconciler converges, and respects its boundary.** Delete the code out from
   under an open, unconsumed entitlement — it is reissued on the next pass. Let an
   entitlement reach `closed` — its code is deleted. Put a code on the channel that no
   `access_invites` row knows about — it is logged once as unmanaged and **left
   alone**. No member, role or channel setting is touched in any pass, asserted
   positively rather than by omission.
7. **Crash between Discord and Postgres.** The worker killed after creating an invite
   but before committing the code, then restarted, converges within one poll interval:
   the next pass finds a channel invite with no matching `access_invites` row, logs it
   unmanaged, and issues the real one. The orphan is never deleted by the app and needs
   no deletion — its own `max_age` expires it no later than window close. **This is
   the reason `max_age` is set at all** rather than relying on the delete call, and the
   reason it must never be `0`.
8. **The 15-minute gap is rejected at the database.** A booking starting less than
   15 minutes after an existing booking's end is refused by the exclusion
   constraint — asserted by inserting directly, bypassing the booking service, so
   the trigger-maintained `reserved` column is exercised too.

- **Transactional email provider** (Resend/Postmark/SES) with sender-domain
  verification. DNS propagation and verification commonly take hours to days, and
  discovering that late would block the mandatory confirmation email and the access
  email behind it. Start this before anything else.
- **Riot production API key application** — free, and approval may not arrive in
  time. Apply anyway.

**Then:**
- **A dedicated Discord server** for coaching, not Alex's community server. Hard
  requirement, for the reason in *Access model*.
- Discord application with OAuth scopes **`identify`, `email`** configured; bot invited
  with **Create Instant Invite** and **Manage Channels** only. No Manage Roles, no Move
  Members, and no role-hierarchy ordering to get right — three classes of silent-403
  bug leave the project with them.
- Discord **member verification / rules screening disabled**, or the `pending` state
  handled explicitly: a `pending` member cannot connect to voice, which would look
  exactly like the access system failing.
- A coaching voice channel, with **no permission overwrites in this release**. The
  server is the access boundary, which is what makes the dedicated server mandatory
  rather than tidy.
- Stripe account in **test mode**; three Price objects created — VOD Review `3000`,
  Live Coaching `4000`, Both `6000`, all USD; webhook endpoint and signing secret
  configured.
- AWS account, S3 bucket, IAM user scoped to that bucket, and a CORS policy
  permitting browser presigned PUTs from the app origin.
- PaaS project plus managed Postgres for the live link.
- Code host (GitHub) and CI pipeline; **`git init`** — the directory is not yet a
  repository, so nothing is under version control and continuous checkpointing
  cannot run.

Throwaway Discord server. One voice channel. Create an invite through the API with

2. **Wait out the two minutes, then click the same link again** from a third account

   disappears from `GET /channels/{id}/invites` on its own. That self-expiry is the
   safety net mandatory test 7 depends on.

Fifteen minutes. Write the answers in the README under failure modes, with the date.

### R3-2 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The new wiring of the Refund-booking control into the disambiguation rules does not match how those rules are written, and the mismatch changes the outcome for the after-window-close case the same paragraph just defined. The control now says it 'records the refund against the booking first, so the resulting Stripe webhook matches a known record and disambiguation rule 1 applies - rule 2's timing fallback is only ever reached by a refund issued outside the app.' But rule 1 is keyed specifically on a cancellation: 'A first-class cancel action on `/ops` ... records the cancellation, and *then* calls the Stripe refund API. The resulting webhook is matched to the recorded cancellation and takes no further action.' Rule 2's trigger is 'A refund that arrives with no preceding cancel record'. The Refund-booking control writes a refund record, not a cancellation record, so by the letter of both rules its webhook falls into rule 2, whose at-or-after-window-start branch prescribes 'refund-during-window (slot consumed, access revoked on the next pass)'. For a refund issued after window close that directly contradicts the control's stated behavior - 'used after window close it leaves the terminal entitlement untouched and moves only money' - because rule 2 would move a `closed` (or `expired_unfulfilled`) entitlement to `revoked`. The rules list is the implementable form of this logic, and it was not updated when the second control was given its own record, so an implementer reading rules 1 and 2 verbatim builds the behavior the control's paragraph says is wrong.

**Remedy**

> Widen rule 1 to the record it actually matches - a refund webhook matched to any preceding in-app record, cancellation or refund, takes no further action - and restate rule 2's trigger as 'a refund that arrives with no preceding in-app cancel or refund record'. Then state once that the entitlement effect for an in-app refund is decided by the Refund-booking control at the time of the action (open window -> `revoked`, closed window -> untouched), so rule 2's timing fallback applies only to refunds issued outside the app.
