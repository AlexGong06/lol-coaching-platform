## Problem Statement

Alex coaches League of Legends players. Today students reach out over Discord DMs,
agree on a time by hand, and pay through an informal channel. There is no booking
surface, no payment gate, and no automatic control over who can enter the Discord
coaching space or when.

A platform is being built to replace that manual flow. It has a second, equally
real purpose: it is the project Alex is submitting to **Corgi** (corgi.insure) for a
software engineer screening on **2026-11-16**, seven days out. Corgi is a full-stack
insurance carrier; its renters-insurance platform Beagle automates enrollment,
billing, compliance, claims, and physical fulfillment. Corgi's job description asks
for state machines with real financial consequences, messy third-party data
ingestion, real money flows, integration-heavy systems with monitoring, and
end-to-end ownership of production systems.

So the deliverable has to satisfy two readers at once: a student who wants to book
and attend a session, and an engineering team deciding whether to hire.

## Demand Evidence

  people before without this platform so the product should absolutely be usable by

  happens and already gets paid for.

What is *not* established: volume. Nothing in this session established how many
students per week, so the design assumes low concurrency and does not invest in
scale. Correctness under concurrency is still enforced at the database level
because it is cheap there, not because volume demands it.

## Status Quo

Students message Alex on Discord, the two of them settle on a time by hand, and
money moves informally. Discord server access is granted manually and never
revoked. Nothing records what was agreed, nothing confirms payment against a
session, and nothing ends access when a session ends.

Every feature in the recommended approach maps to a specific failure of this
workflow. That mapping is the README's opening paragraph, and it is the most
credible framing the project has: this replaces something real, and the thing it
replaces can be described precisely.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Two users, one system.**

1. **The student** — a League player who wants a one-hour session, picks a slot,
   pays, and gets into the coaching voice channel at the right time without
   messaging anyone.
2. **The evaluator** — a Corgi engineer who reads the repository, may click a live
   link, and is deciding whether Alex can own a production system where mistakes
   cost money.

**Narrowest wedge:** a student can go from landing page to *inside the voice
channel at the right time* without Alex touching anything. That single path —
book, authorize Discord, pay, receive access at window open, lose access at window
close — is the whole product. Everything else is optional.

## Approaches Considered

### SKU semantics

All three SKUs are **scheduled, synchronous sessions**. They differ only in duration
and in whether a replay upload is expected beforehand. Everything else — slot,
window, Discord role, refund policy, fulfillment — is identical.

| SKU | Duration | Consumes a slot? | Window | Discord role | `.rofl` upload | Fulfilled when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Live Coaching** | 1 h | Yes | = the booking | Yes | No | Window closes |
| **VOD Review** | 1 h | Yes | = the booking | Yes | Expected | Window closes |
| **Both** | 2 h, contiguous | Yes | = the booking | Yes | Expected | Window closes |

**The upload is not a precondition.** If the replay has not arrived by session
start, the session runs anyway and part of it is spent helping the student locate
and send the file — Alex's stated intent, and the reason this design has no
missing-upload fallback branch, no deadline check, and no automatic refund. A
reminder at T-24 h asks for the file in advance so the full hour goes to the review,
but nothing is gated on it.

Consequently there is **one refund policy for all three SKUs** (see Cancellation),
one fulfillment rule (the window closed), and no review state machine.

**`.rofl` only.** The upload accepts League replay files and nothing else: roughly
**10-40 MB** for a full game, one `PUT` to a presigned S3 URL, no multipart (S3
accepts single PUTs to 5 GB), no video transcoding. At that size a minimal
spinner is enough and no progress bar is built, but the figure is stated so the
choice is checkable rather than assumed. The `upload_grant` row authorizes PUTs from payment until **session end
plus one hour**, because students may well upload during the session itself. Known
limitation worth stating in the README: a `.rofl` only opens in a League client on a
matching patch, so a sufficiently old replay may be unplayable — a domain
constraint, not a system failure.

### Booking, holds, and overlap

- Availability is stored as **continuous open ranges** (a recurring rule in Alex's
  timezone), **edited by direct SQL or a seed row for this release** — no admin UI
  (Open Question 4). Continuous ranges rather than pre-cut one-hour slots, because
  the Both SKU is a contiguous 2-hour booking and cannot be assembled from fixed
  1-hour cells.
- **SKU selection comes before the calendar.** The picker cannot search availability
  until it knows whether it needs a 1-hour or a 2-hour contiguous opening, so the
  flow is: choose SKU → choose time → pay.
- **Empty-day messaging for the Both SKU (decided).** When a day holds open time but
  no contiguous 2-hour block, the day renders **empty with an explicit per-day note:
  "No continuous 2-hour blocks on this day."** Per-day, not a global banner, so the
  student can see at a glance which days are worth checking. Without it a fragmented
  calendar is indistinguishable from a broken one — and this is the only place in the
  product where "no availability" has a cause the student would otherwise have to
  guess at.
- A **15-minute gap** is reserved after every booking, enforced by the database (see
  below), so two windows can never be open at once and a departing student cannot
  overlap the next session even with the revoke lagging a full poll interval.
- **Availability search applies the same padding as the constraint, on both sides.**
  A start time is offered only if `[start, end + 15 min)` overlaps no existing
  `pending_payment`/`paid`/`active` booking's reserved range. Without this the
  picker would cheerfully offer 3:05 after a 2:00-3:00 booking and the insert would
  then be refused by the database. The Both SKU's "continuous 2-hour block" test
  includes the trailing gap, so the committed empty-day note is accurate rather than
  optimistic.
- **A constraint rejection is not a concurrency loss, and says so.** "That slot was
  just taken" is reserved for a genuine race. A rejection the search should have
  prevented shows "That time is no longer available — the calendar has been
  refreshed", reloads availability, and is logged as a search/constraint
  disagreement, because recurring instances mean the search is wrong.
- **Minimum lead time: 45 minutes, enforced twice.** The picker offers only future
  occurrences and nothing starting sooner than 45 minutes from now — and the floor is
  **re-checked inside the transaction that creates the hold and the Checkout
  session**, measured from that moment, not from when the page was rendered. Search
  alone is not enough: a picker left open for twenty minutes would otherwise walk a
  stale slot straight past the floor. A stale start reuses the "That time is no
  longer available — the calendar has been refreshed" path. Measuring the floor from
  Checkout-session creation is what keeps it strictly longer than the session's own
  30-minute life. This is
  load-bearing, not politeness: the Checkout session lives 30 minutes, so a slot
  starting in 5 minutes could be paid for after its own window had already opened —
  leaving a student who paid for a session that is partly or wholly in the past, and
  non-refundable on creation under the 24-hour rule. Bookings cannot be made inside
  the window, so `expired_unfulfilled` always means a platform failure and its
  automatic refund stays correct.
- **A lapsed hold is swept by the poller.** The exclusion constraint keys on
  `state`, not on a timestamp, so an abandoned Checkout would block its slot *and*
  its gap forever until something writes the terminal state. At hold creation a
  due-work row is enqueued for the hold's expiry instant; the same ~30-second poller
  claims it with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and transitions the booking to
  `expired_hold`. The slot is not re-offered until that transition lands.
- **The hold is created when the Stripe Checkout session is created, not at slot
  selection.** Stripe measures its 30-minute minimum `expires_at` from *session
  creation*; a hold started earlier at slot selection would always need an
  `expires_at` less than 30 minutes away and Stripe would reject it. So: picking a
  slot in the UI reserves nothing, clicking through to pay creates the hold and the
  Checkout session in one transaction, and the hold is set to expire **60 seconds
  after** the Checkout session. Stripe therefore refuses payment before the slot is
  released, which closes the *authorization* race — a new payment cannot start once
  the slot is free. It does **not** close webhook-delivery lag: a payment completed
  at minute 29 can deliver after minute 31, which is exactly the deliberate remainder
  the Expired-hold race handler below exists to absorb. Two students on
  the picker at once is resolved first-click-wins, and the loser is told the slot
  was just taken.
- Overlap and double-booking are prevented **in the database**, by a Postgres
  exclusion constraint:

  ```sql
  -- A stored, trigger-maintained range. NOT a generated column and NOT an
  -- inline expression: see the note below.
  ALTER TABLE bookings ADD COLUMN reserved tstzrange;

  CREATE FUNCTION bookings_set_reserved() RETURNS trigger AS $$
  BEGIN
    NEW.reserved := CASE WHEN NEW.starts_at IS NULL THEN NULL
      ELSE tstzrange(NEW.starts_at, NEW.ends_at + interval '15 minutes') END;
    RETURN NEW;
  END $$ LANGUAGE plpgsql;

  CREATE TRIGGER bookings_reserved BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE ON bookings
    FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION bookings_set_reserved();

  ALTER TABLE bookings ADD CONSTRAINT bookings_no_overlap
    EXCLUDE USING gist (reserved WITH &&)
    WHERE (reserved IS NOT NULL
           AND state IN ('pending_payment','paid','active'));
  ```

  **Why a trigger and not the obvious inline expression.** The natural form,
  `EXCLUDE USING gist (tstzrange(starts_at, ends_at + interval '15 minutes') WITH &&)`,
  **does not compile.** An `EXCLUDE` constraint builds an index, PostgreSQL requires
  index expressions to be `IMMUTABLE`, and `timestamptz + interval` is only `STABLE`
  — adding `'1 day'` and `'24 hours'` to a timestamptz give different answers across
  a DST boundary, so the operator's volatility is per-function, not per-argument. The
  migration would fail with *"functions in index expression must be marked
  IMMUTABLE."* Wrapping the arithmetic in a hand-declared `IMMUTABLE` function would
  work and is a known trick — safe here only because a fixed 15-minute interval has
  no day or month component — but lying to the planner about volatility can silently
  corrupt an index, and it is a bad thing to have to defend in an interview. The
  trigger keeps the derivation in the database, where a buggy application write
  cannot bypass it, and indexes a plain stored column.

  **The gap lives inside the reserved range, so the database enforces it.** Book
  2:00-3:00 and Postgres reserves 2:00-3:15; nothing can start before 3:15. The gap
  — not the overlap check — is what keeps a departing student out of the next session
  while revoke lags up to a poll interval, and that guarantee should not depend on
  slot-generation code being correct. One constraint covers duplicate bookings,
  overlapping windows, variable durations, and the gap.

  `reserved IS NOT NULL` is defensive rather than load-bearing now that every SKU is
  a scheduled session: `tstzrange(NULL, NULL)` is *not* null but the unbounded range
  `(,)`, which overlaps everything, so a future windowless product would otherwise
  block the whole table.
- **Timezones:** every window stored as a UTC instant, availability stored as a rule
  in Alex's timezone, rendered in the viewer's, both shown on confirmation.

### Booking and entitlement states

One enumerated set, referenced by the exclusion constraint predicate, the
cancellation table, the reconciler's notion of an open window, and the README
diagram.

**Booking states:** `pending_payment` → `paid` → `active` → `completed`. Terminal
branches: `expired_hold` (hold lapsed unpaid, written by the poller),
`payment_orphaned` (paid but the slot was lost), `cancelled_refunded`,
`cancelled_no_refund`, `refund_failed` (refund retries exhausted; needs a human).
There is no separate `held` state — the hold and the Checkout session are created in
one transaction, so the row is never observable without a session attached, and
`pending_payment` is the initial state.

**Entitlement states:** `pending` (created, window not yet open) → `open` (window
current, role should be held) → `closed` (window ended normally). Terminal
branches: `voided` (never opened), `revoked` (cut short by refund or late
cancellation), `expired_unfulfilled` (window passed without the student ever
getting in).

**Supporting states:** OAuth token `live` | `dead`; `upload_grant` `open` | `closed`;
`access_events` is append-only and includes `fallback_issued`. There are no review
states, because fulfillment is window closure for every SKU.

**Which definition of "open" is authoritative:** the **stored state** governs the
reconciler's diff; the **timestamps** govern the transition that sets it. The worker
moves an entitlement `pending → open` at window start and `open → closed` at window
end, and the diff grants the role to entitlements in state `open` and nothing else.
This matters in two cases the design depends on: a window that has started but whose
row the worker has not yet flipped grants nothing (correct — never early), and a
refund inside a current window sets `revoked`, which the diff stops granting
immediately even though the clock still says the window is open.

### Payment-to-entitlement

- **Stripe Checkout (hosted)**, `expires_at` = creation + 30 minutes, with the hold
  expiring 60 seconds later, so no card data ever touches the app.
- The webhook handler is the **only** writer that creates an entitlement:
  signature-verified, idempotent on `event.id` via a `processed_events` table,
  inside one transaction with the booking state transition.
- **Expired-hold race:** if a webhook arrives for a booking whose hold lapsed, the
  handler re-checks the slot. Still free → reseat and proceed. Taken → **immediate
  full refund**, booking → `payment_orphaned`, student emailed, row raised red in
  `/ops`. This is a system fault, so the >24h/<24h policy does not apply; it is
  always a full refund.
- **Checkout return page:** shows "confirming payment" and polls booking state. If
  the entitlement does not exist after 60 seconds it tells the student the
  confirmation email is authoritative and stops spinning.

### Access model: roles primary, invite fallback

- **One shared role, `coaching-active`**, with a single permanent permission
  overwrite on the coaching voice channel: `@everyone` denied both View Channel and
  Connect, `coaching-active` allowed both. A shared role is safe precisely because
  the 15-minute inter-slot gap and the exclusion constraint guarantee
  non-overlapping windows; per-booking roles would add channel-overwrite churn for
  no isolation benefit.
- **Grant: never before window start, and within one poll interval after it**
  (~30s, worst case 60s). There is no early grace of any kind. The worker refreshes
  the OAuth token if needed, adds the member via `guilds.join`, then grants the role.
- **Revoke: no later than one poll interval after window end** (~30s, worst case
  60s). Two calls: remove the role, **and**
  `PATCH /guilds/{g}/members/{u}` with `channel_id: null` to force-disconnect.
  **Never kick.** The second call ships unconditionally because it is idempotent
  and cheap; the D0 probe only records the observed behavior in the README.
  If the force-disconnect returns **403**, the role removal still proceeds (so the
  student cannot rejoin), the failure is written to `access_events`, and the row is
  raised red in `/ops` for Alex to disconnect by hand.
- **The only timing tolerance is poller latency.** It is a measured property of the
  architecture, not a policy: access never opens early, and it closes within one
  poll interval of the window end. The 15-minute inter-slot gap exists precisely to
  absorb that lag (see Open Question 8).
- **Invite fallback.** If `guilds.join` fails at window open, the worker
  immediately (a) emails the student a single-use invite with `max_age` set to the
  remaining window seconds, (b) surfaces the same invite on the booking page, and
  (c) writes a `fallback_issued` event. When the student joins, the reconciler's
  next pass sees a guild member with an open entitlement and grants
  `coaching-active` — no separate code path is needed, because the reconciler
  derives desired state from entitlements and actual state from the guild. If the
  invite is never used before the window closes, the entitlement terminates as
  `expired_unfulfilled`, `/ops` flags it, and **Alex issues a full refund**, because
  the failure was ours.

### Reconciler, not scheduler

- A Postgres due-work table polled every ~30 seconds with
  `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`.
- Each pass computes the desired `coaching-active` holder set from entitlements **in
  state `open`**, fetches actual guild state, applies the diff,
  backs off on 429, and appends every action and failure to `access_events`.
- **Bounded authority, with the two directions bounded differently.** The
  reconciler touches **only** the role named `coaching-active` and never any other
  role, member attribute or channel. Within that role:
  - **Grants** go only to members holding an `open` entitlement.
  - **Removals** apply to **every holder of `coaching-active` who does not currently
    hold an open entitlement** — whether or not they have an entitlement record at
    all. This is the security-relevant direction, so it cannot be narrowed to known
    bookings: a role hand-granted by Alex, left behind by a deleted booking, or
    granted during an incident would otherwise keep View Channel and Connect on the
    coaching voice channel forever, including during someone else's paid session.
  - A permanent allowlist, `RECONCILER_EXEMPT_DISCORD_IDS`, holds Alex's own account
    and the bot, and nothing else.
- **Token rotation protocol.** Refreshes happen only inside a claimed due-work row,
  so two passes can never refresh the same user concurrently; the claim takes
  `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on that user's token row as well. The new token is
  persisted and committed **before** it is used for `guilds.join`. If the refresh
  response is lost, the transaction rolls back and the old token is retained; if a
  later refresh returns `invalid_grant`, the token is marked `dead`, an event is
  written, and the booking routes straight to the invite fallback.

### Cancellation, refunds, and no-shows

Separate concerns, each with defined behavior:

| Event | Slot | Money | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student cancels **>24h** before | Released | Full refund | Entitlement voided before it ever opens |
| Student cancels **<24h** before (before window start) | Released | **No refund** | Entitlement `voided` before it opens, so no access is ever granted |
| **Alex cancels, before window start** (`/ops` cancel booking) | Released | Full refund, enqueued by the app after the slot is released | Entitlement `voided`; student emailed |
| **Alex refunds at or after window start** (`/ops` refund booking) | Consumed | Full refund, enqueued | Open window → entitlement `revoked`, role removed and force-disconnect on the next pass; closed window → terminal entitlement untouched |
| **No-show** (window elapses unused) | Consumed | No refund | Access was granted and available; booking → `completed` |
| **Refund during an open window** (Alex-initiated or dispute) | Consumed | Refund per Stripe | Entitlement → `revoked`; next pass removes the role **and** force-disconnects within one poll interval; booking page shows a banner; student emailed |

**How the system tells an Alex-cancellation from a refund-during-window.** These two
rows prescribe opposite slot outcomes and a bare Stripe refund webhook carries no
intent, so the signal is stated rather than inferred:

1. **A first-class cancel action on `/ops`** is the committed path. It releases the
   slot, voids the entitlement, records the cancellation, and *then* calls the Stripe
   refund API. The resulting webhook is matched to the recorded cancellation and
   takes no further action.
2. **A refund that arrives with no preceding cancel record** — issued straight from
   the Stripe dashboard, or a dispute — falls back to timing: **before** window start
   is treated as a cancellation (slot released); **at or after** window start is
   treated as refund-during-window (slot consumed, access revoked on the next pass).

The visible policy line, identical for all three SKUs: **full refund more than 24
hours before the session, no refund inside 24 hours, and a full refund any time the
platform fails to deliver access.** No SKU-specific carve-outs, because all three are
scheduled sessions.

### Operations and notifications

- **`/ops` page, really authenticated.** Discord OAuth plus an owner-ID allowlist
  (`OPS_OWNER_DISCORD_IDS`); the previously planned env var remains **only** as an
  additional kill switch, because an env var controls whether a route exists, not
  who may reach it. The page shows booking state, window, last reconcile timestamp,
  and a red list of failed actions with retry — which covers **both** reconciler
  actions and failed refunds. It carries two operator controls:

  - **Cancel booking** — releases the slot, voids the entitlement, records the
    cancellation, then enqueues the refund. Available only before window start.
  - **Refund booking** — for a window that is already open or has closed, where
    there is no slot to release. Its effect on the entitlement depends on which:
    **used during an open window** it sets the entitlement to `revoked`, so the next
    reconcile pass removes the role and force-disconnects (this is the behavior
    mandatory test 5 asserts); **used after window close** it leaves the terminal
    entitlement untouched and moves only money. Either way it records the refund
    against the booking first, so the resulting Stripe webhook matches a known record
    and disambiguation rule 1 applies — rule 2's timing fallback is only ever reached
    by a refund issued outside the app. This control exists because the design
    commits to a refund in exactly that situation: an `expired_unfulfilled`
    entitlement, where the student never got in and the failure was ours.

  No review-marking controls exist, because fulfillment is window closure for every
  SKU. The page displays a student's Discord username and the Stripe payment-intent
  ID only — never card data, which never reaches the app, and never an email address.

- **Refunds are durable work, not a fire-and-forget API call.** The cancel path
  records the cancellation with a `refund_pending` marker and enqueues the Stripe
  refund as a due-work row; the poller retries it with backoff. Sequencing the refund
  last means a failed call would otherwise leave a student with no session, no
  entitlement and no money — the single worst state this system can produce. A
  refund that exhausts its retries lands in state `refund_failed` and sits red in the
  `/ops` list until a human clears it.

- **Students cancel from the booking page**, which is authenticated by their Discord
  login and linked from the confirmation email. The control disappears at window
  start: once the session is open, the policy outcome is identical to a no-show and
  there is nothing useful left to cancel.
- **Confirmation email** with `.ics` attached, plus a T-24 h reminder that also asks
  VOD and Both students to send their `.rofl` ahead of time so the full session goes
  to the review. **For a booking made less than 24 hours out**, the T-24 h trigger is
  already past: the file request is folded into the confirmation email and the
  separate reminder is suppressed. The visible policy states that such a booking is
  non-refundable from the moment it is made. Mandatory because real students will pay once live mode is enabled,
  and silence after payment reads as failure — exactly the manual-DM loop this
  platform exists to remove.
- **S3 presigned upload** for `.rofl` files, authorized by an `upload_grant` row
  valid from payment until session end plus one hour.

## Mandatory tests (the floor; CI runs all eight on push)

**Test strategy, because five of these touch third parties.** Discord is a **fake
client** implementing the four calls used (`guilds.join`, role add, role remove,
force-disconnect) over an in-memory guild model; Stripe webhooks are **replayed
fixtures** posted to the real handler with real signature verification against a
test secret. Tests 3 and 8 run against a **real PostgreSQL** service container,
because they assert database behavior and a double would prove nothing. No test needs
live Discord or Stripe credentials, so the whole suite runs on push. A manual
smoke script exercises the real APIs once per deploy, separately from CI.
**Time is a double too:** due-at timestamps and window boundaries are seeded relative
to the test clock and the poller is invoked directly for a single pass, so no test
waits out a 31-minute hold expiry or a 60-second poll interval.

1. **Hold expiry transitions and releases.** After the hold's expiry instant the
   poller writes `expired_hold`, and a second booking for the same range then
   succeeds. Asserted as a state transition and an ordering — not as Stripe's live
   refusal, which cannot occur inside a push build given the 30-minute floor.
2. A replayed Stripe webhook creates exactly one entitlement.
3. Two simultaneous bookings for the same time: one succeeds, one is rejected by the
   exclusion constraint. A 2-hour Both booking is rejected when it would span an
   existing 1-hour booking, and accepted into a clear 2-hour-plus-gap opening.
4. **Boundary behavior as inequalities, not instants:** no grant exists before window
   start; the grant is observed within 60 s after window start; the revoke is
   observed within 60 s after window end.
5. A refund during an open window sets `revoked` and access is removed within one
   poll interval, even though the clock still says the window is open.
6. **The reconciler converges in both directions.** Hand-remove the role from a
   student with an open entitlement — it comes back. Hand-grant the role to a member
   with *no booking at all* — it is removed. An account in
   `RECONCILER_EXEMPT_DISCORD_IDS` keeps it. No other role on any member changes.
7. The worker killed mid-grant and restarted converges to correct access within one
   poll interval.
8. **The 15-minute gap is rejected at the database.** A booking starting less than
   15 minutes after an existing booking's end is refused by the exclusion
   constraint — asserted by inserting directly, bypassing the booking service, so
   the trigger-maintained `reserved` column is exercised too.

### R2-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The new exclusion constraint is predicated on every booking row having a time window, but the new SKU table commits to a VOD Review purchase with "Entitlement window: None" and "Consumes a slot?: No". If VOD rows live in the same table, their window columns are null, and in PostgreSQL `tstzrange(NULL, NULL)` is not NULL — it is the unbounded range `(,)`, which the exclusion constraint will index (nulls are only skipped when the whole key expression is null) and which overlaps every other range. The predicate `WHERE (state IN ('held','paid','active'))` covers paid rows, so the first paid VOD Review booking would collide with every live booking and with every other paid VOD booking, making the second VOD purchase impossible. The document never says whether VOD purchases are rows in the constrained table, whether they carry a synthetic window, or whether they live in a separate table.

**Remedy**

> State where VOD Review and "Both" purchases are recorded relative to the exclusion constraint. Either exclude windowless rows from the constraint explicitly (add `AND starts_at IS NOT NULL` to the WHERE clause, or make the key expression null for windowless rows), or put slot-consuming bookings in their own table. Also name the full set of booking states the predicate's `'held','paid','active'` is drawn from, since `active` appears nowhere else in the document.

### R2-2 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> This mechanism is not satisfiable under the constraint the document itself added two sections earlier: "Stripe Checkout `expires_at` has a **30-minute minimum**". The minimum is measured from Checkout Session creation, and the hold starts at slot selection, which is strictly earlier than the moment the student reaches Checkout. Inputs: hold = 30 minutes from slot selection; session created at slot selection + d for any d > 0; required expires_at = slot selection + 30 min. Result: expires_at is only (30 - d) minutes after creation, i.e. below the 30-minute floor, so Stripe rejects it for every d > 0. **Remedy**

> Resolve the coupling explicitly: either start the 30-minute hold at Checkout Session creation (not at slot selection) so the two expiries coincide, or make the hold longer than 30 minutes (for example 35) and set `expires_at` to creation + 30 minutes, and state which of the hold and the session expires first.
